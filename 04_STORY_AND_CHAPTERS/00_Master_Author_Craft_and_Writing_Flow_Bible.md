## 1. CORE PHILOSOPHY: WHY MODERN ANIME / OTT STYLE?

Traditional Indian novels and 90s TV serials rely on heavy, formal, textbook Hindi (`मुख्य अभियंता`, `प्रतीत हो रहा था`, `अत्यंत क्रोधित होकर`), which creates distance between the reader and a 21st-century 19-year-old protagonist.

*Brahmand* is written with the pacing, dialogue rhythm, and sensory immersion of:
1. **Modern Hindi Anime Dubs** (*Jujutsu Kaisen*, *Solo Leveling*, *One Punch Man*, *MCU Hindi*): Zero forced translation of technical or everyday English words; fast, punchy dialogue beats; natural reactions (`Wait...`, `Seriously?`, `Chill कर`, `What the hell`).
2. **Prestige Indian OTT Series** (*The Family Man*, *Farzi*, *Mirzapur*): Character-driven code-switching; show-don't-tell action beats instead of flowery emotional summaries.
3. **Modern Mytho-Sci-Fi Cinema** (*Kalki 2898 AD*, *Tumbbad*, *Baahubali*): Weighty, atmospheric Hindi for cosmic/mythological scenes that hits hard without requiring a Sanskrit dictionary.

## 2. THE FIVE NON-NEGOTIABLE WRITING RULES

### Rule 1: The Dubbing / Read-Aloud Test
Read every sentence aloud. If it sounds like a Netflix/Prime series or Crunchyroll Hindi dub, keep it. If it sounds like a school Hindi essay or Doordarshan bulletin, rewrite it immediately.

### Rule 2: Dual-Script Hinglish Lock
- Write **Hindi words in Devanagari** (`सपना`, `दीवार`, `बारिश`).
- Write **English words, technical terms, names, and modern idioms in English Latin script** (`Neel`, `RITA`, `workbench`, `hydrolock`, `sports coupe`, `air pressure`, `battery`, `smirk`, `blank`).
- Never transliterate English words into awkward Devanagari inside modern POV scenes (`हायड्रोलॉक` -> `hydrolock`; `एस्प्रेसो` -> `espresso`).

### Rule 3: The 3-Register Voice Architecture
1. **Cosmic & High Mythos (*Kalki / Baahubali* Register):** Clean, weighty, poetic Hindi for Prologue Part 1 and High Devas. No opaque dictionary Sanskrit.
2. **Folk & Universe B Streets (*Tumbbad / Mirzapur* Register):** Blunt, grounded, sensory Hindi for Prologue Part 2 and Central City guards/merchants (`मर्त्य`, `पट्टिका`, `तांब्री`).
3. **Neel & RITA POV (*JJK Dub / Farzi* + *TMKOC Rita Reporter* Register):**
   - **Neel:** Sharp South Mumbai Hinglish, engineer mindset, short clipped thoughts.
   - **RITA:** Live TV field-reporter energy (TMKOC Rita Reporter) in modern Hinglish, rotating live sign-ins, invisible-mic reactions, audience framing, and cameraman jokes without repeating catchphrases.

### Rule 4: Cinematic Pacing & Show-Don't-Tell
- **Action / Shock Beats:** 1–2 line paragraphs for fast visual cuts.
- **Zero Adjective Stacking:** Never stack 3–4 heavy adjectives (`अगाध, असीम, निस्तब्ध`) in a single sentence. One concrete sensory detail beats four abstract adjectives.
- **Zero Dossier Dumps:** Never pause a scene to explain character backstories (e.g., Uncle Kani's boardroom politics or why Neel programmed RITA) in a Wikipedia paragraph. Reveal it organically through dialogue and subtext.
- **Zero Spoiler Narration:** Never break Limited POV with omniscient lines like *"उसे नहीं पता था कि वह आखिरी बार लैब देख रहा था।"*

### Rule 5: Strict Orthography & Continuity
- **Canonical `'नही'` Rule:** Always write **`नही`** (no anusvara dot) everywhere.
- **Numbers:** Use digits for telemetry, timestamps, currency, and stats (`36 घंटे`, `108 BPM`, `18%`, `4 करोड़`, `₹15`, `0.05 second`).
- **Limited POV & Narrative Veil:** Neel never knows Universe B lore upfront. The 0.05s Dadar transit is a brief, *Interstellar*-style disorienting gravity/spatial fold lasting only a few seconds; Neel blacks out immediately and wakes up in the manure cart with **zero memory** of the transit, ending Chapter 1 on a pure stunned cliffhanger (`"What the... hell?"`).
